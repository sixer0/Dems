| Why an acknowledgement did nothing | acknowledging sets `acknowledged`, never `active` (by design) | gate 4.5 |

**The rule implemented.** The restart fault is active only while the session it was raised for is still open as that faulted session: the open session carries the fault itself (`session.fault.coreNotRunning === true`) and is in FAULT, RECOVERY or FINALIZING. In every other state of the engine - the session SEALED, no session, the next session open - the fault is cleared with one `FAULT_CLEARED` row and its alert resolved. **Why the session and not "the Core runs again":** the fault does not say the Core is down; it says a session was open while the Core was not running and that its recording ended there. While that session waits for its seal (or its seal is blocked) the statement is about something still open; once it is sealed as interrupted or closed, nothing is left that the fault is about. It is the rule gate 75 applied to the capture fault of a killed worker (G75-10 (a)).

Where it runs: in the unit of `completeSeal` and of `endSessionUnsealable` (the clear row commits with the end of the session), and at every process start after the start's own raise (the heal of a stored stuck fault: lane 2's `ALR-000016` is cleared at the lane's next start, no reset). The hosted `recover` keeps the simulator's own clear (a session closed INCOMPLETE). Idempotent: a state without such a fault is returned as it is.

Known limit, stated: the `FAULT_CLEARED` row carries `sessionId` of the session that is CURRENT when it is written (the shared `clearFaultAtKey` does that for every clear). In the unit of the seal that is the faulted session. At the heal of lane 2 it will be the lane's current session (a later, sealed one), not `SES-20261010-0028`. Nothing reads it as the fault's session; the extract of a sealed package is not touched.
